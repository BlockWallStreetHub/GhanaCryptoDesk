Exchanges, Wallets & Bitcoin Merchants

How Ghanaians buy and sell Bitcoin with Mobile Money, where to keep it safe, which tools merchants use, and the local circular-economy communities already spending it.

Check the licence first. Under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025 (Act 1154), exchanges, wallet providers and other crypto businesses must register or be licensed with the Bank of Ghana and SEC. When we researched this page (November 2026) we could not find a single public, consolidated list of licensed platforms, so a listing here is not a licence check and not an endorsement. Confirm a company's status with the Bank of Ghana Virtual Assets Department (vasp@bog.gov.gh · bog.gov.gh/virtual-assets) before you deposit.

How to buy Bitcoin with Mobile Money (the safe way)

Verify your account. Most platforms need ID verification (KYC) before you can deposit cedis. Use your real name — the name on your Mobile Money wallet must match.
Do a tiny test first. Buy GH₵50–100, then withdraw it to your own wallet. Confirm the whole route works before moving larger amounts.
On P2P, follow the escrow. Pick sellers with high completion rates and many trades. Pay only the account shown inside the order, in the right amount, and never click “paid” before you actually pay.
Never release Bitcoin early. When selling, release only after the cedis show in your Mobile Money app — not after a screenshot or an SMS someone forwards you.
Withdraw to a wallet you control and copy the Bitcoin address carefully. Send a small test amount first. Bitcoin transactions cannot be reversed.

Reality check on spending Bitcoin

Most Ghanaians today use Bitcoin as savings and cash it out to Mobile Money when needed. Direct spending at shops is growing — especially inside community circular economies like Bitcoin Dua in Agbozume — but it is still niche. Where a business accepts Bitcoin, it usually uses a Lightning wallet or a payment processor that settles in cedis.
